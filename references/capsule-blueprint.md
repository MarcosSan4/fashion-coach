# Capsule blueprint (generic men's core wardrobe)

Used after closet intake to seed his shopping file: compare his closet against this, **translated into his palette, frame and formality life**, and write the gaps plus a priority queue. Not a shopping list to buy wholesale: most men already own half of it.

## Philosophy

A lean core where almost everything combines, because it shares his palette and one fit language (his signature silhouette). The maths is the point: 7 tops x 5 bottoms = 35 combinations before layers and shoes. Every piece earns its place by pairing with most others (A).

## Target capsule (translate every colour into his registers)

- **Tops:** 3-4 well-cut tees (his lightest neutral, his darkest neutral, a mid neutral, one earth or hero); 2-3 knit polos (one neutral, one light, one hero); 2 shirts (white + a mid tone, for the dressier register); 1 linen or camp shirt if his climate has a real summer; 2 fine knits (one dark neutral, one hero).
- **Bottoms:** 2 jeans (dark wash = dressiest, plus a mid wash); 2 chinos or trousers in his cut (one earth, one neutral); 1 tailored trouser (dark neutral); 1 tailored short if his summer needs it.
- **Layers:** 1 bridge jacket (overshirt, chore jacket, Harrington or bomber: fills the "blazer too dressy, coat too heavy" gap); 1 unstructured blazer in a dark neutral (the single biggest formality unlock); a coat only if his coldest climate band needs one.
- **Shoes:** clean white leather sneakers (the universal connector); one leather shoe (loafers, Chelseas or derbies) at formality 4; one character sneaker for relaxed bottoms if his aesthetic is street-leaning.
- **Accessories:** a watch in his metal, belts matching his shoes, sunglasses in a frame shape that suits him.

## Frame adjustments

- **Slim / rectangle:** prioritise structure up top (overshirt, blazer, collared knits) and textured fabrics.
- **Athletic V:** prioritise relaxed bottoms in his cut and tops cut to the chest; slim fits are a trap.
- **Broad / stocky, heavier midsection:** prioritise open layers that create a vertical line and dark, tonal bottoms; skip chunky knits.
- **Short:** prioritise tonal sets (matching top and bottom colours), high-rise bottoms, shorter jackets.
- **Tall:** check sleeve and inseam on everything; layers and wider legs work.

## Writing the gaps and the queue

For each blueprint slot: owned (name the piece), partial (owned but off-palette, ill-fitting or end-of-life), or gap. Then rank the gaps by **leverage**, adjusted by anything his finale grades taught you about his taste (a piece he finds costume-like drops): how many new outfits the piece unlocks with what he already owns (the combinability test), then how often his formality life needs it.

Typical high-leverage order, when the gap exists:
0. **A coat, if his coldest climate band needs one and he owns none.** Nothing else matters for half the year without it.
1. The missing formality unlock (usually the unstructured blazer, or the one leather shoe).
2. A dark-wash jean or dark trouser if his bottoms all skew light.
3. The bridge jacket.
4. A hero piece if his closet has none (one, not five).
5. Fine knit in a neutral or hero.
6. Replace-on-wear basics (tees, white sneakers).

**Invest vs save:** spend where it shows and takes wear (shoes, outerwear, blazer, knitwear, hard-to-fit denim and trousers: about 40-50% of the budget); save on high-churn basics (tees, simple layers) whose cost-per-wear cycle is short regardless of price (A).

**Sourcing:** use the stores he named in onboarding first; add his region's reliable options by tier (save: Uniqlo, Arket, H&M premium lines; mid: COS, Massimo Dutti, J.Crew, Banana Republic; invest: per his aesthetic's reference brands). **A good tailor is often the highest-leverage purchase:** buy to the widest point, have the rest taken in.
